the notebook, the commit history, the specs, the landing page draft and the session notes.
Nothing here is decided. Where a way to solve something came up, it is listed as what it is,

## 1. What is fixed

Three statements, in the README since 3 Nov:

1. Writing costs nothing. One gesture, no categories, no decisions, no audience.
2. Your words are yours. Only you write them. Anyone else writes beside them, or on a page
   of their own, and can be removed.
3. Meaning comes later. What a line is, what it connects to, what it should trigger, is
   decided after it is written, and only once it has earned it.

Behind them, the hypothesis (3 Nov): thoughts are shaped by the environment they are written
in. A capture surface with no load, no format, no audience and no algorithm should produce
more reflective, observing thinking. Capture first, not prompt first. The owner never files
anything; every label is placed afterwards by someone else.

The author's rules (spec/weeju.md): a line belongs to whoever wrote it; every action is an
event under an id, with where it ran, visible in History and Logs; the owner takes anything
back with one hold; nobody has a name until they choose one.

## 2. Direction, in the owner's words

- **An operating system for a person's life and work, where everything begins with
  capture** (5 Nov 17:37, 17:46; spec/system.md). The use cases are evidence of components,
  not features. Great tools fit many people and are easy to use (17:47).
- **Integration itself is a feature.** "Infinite integrations for your specific needs that
  bring it together in a single shared space via programmable plugins" (4 Nov 17:17).
  "Anyone should be able to add what they need or engineer loops/pipelines/inputs on the go
  with 0 friction" (5 Nov 17:42).
- **A habit with no forced outcome.** Capturing commits you to nothing, yet when you want a
  sale next week, a site can use all the related captures to tell the story, build
  inventory, take orders (5 Nov 17:44).
- **Companion, not competitor.** A capture layer that adds to people's existing Claude,
  Grok or ChatGPT (6 Nov 12:46).
- **Cost of intelligence.** Calling model APIs puts the bill on us per user; today the
  owner's own Claude subscription is the intelligence, so the bill is zero at the cost of
  convenience (6 Nov 12:43).
- **Network effects.** Google and Meta own people's captures and generational databases
  (maps, contacts). Locations here are bare coordinates. A shared layer across dottos, such
  as public places, needs thought on incentives (6 Nov 12:48).
- **Onboarding burden.** Historical state (a whole bookshelf, past expenses) must not be
  demanded before the tool helps; habit forms forward (5 Nov 08:28).
- **The name.** Margin wrong (3 Nov 11:50, 15:46); Capture considered, then felt limiting
  (5 Nov 08:36, 17:46); dotto chosen 6 Nov. Code and bundle ids still say margin and weeju.
- **Today's plan** (8 Nov 09:52): buy the domain; rebrand, rename the apps, a new logo and
  design language if needed, as design research; bring the communication docs up to date
  for consistency.
- **A second milestone** (8 Nov 10:07): any user inside the app can "vibe code" an
  integration or loop, say adding faceit CS2 games, by asking right there, and it is done;
  people reuse each other's loops; agents browse libraries for inspiration, trust and
  reference.
- **Anyone connects their own agent or coding environment** the way we do through Claude
  Code, not only to label but to make loops and even change the app itself; the question
  is the degree of access, a sandboxed mini-app with some outside calls, Roblox as a

- **dotto competes with the iPhone itself on capture.** A food photo went to Camera, not
  dotto, so it would be in Photos and not in an app that might be dropped (7 Nov 14:37).
- **Against loss.** A paper notebook of ideas was thrown out by a hotel cleaner; dotto
  could prevent that (8 Nov 08:11). A lab for experiments to understand the world, with
  tags and code names per project (8 Nov 08:11, 08:16).
- **The milestone** (6 Nov): dotto.works is itself made by dotto from the notebook. If it
  serves our own needs it can serve others. The landing page draft in site/index.html is
  the statement of the ideal product; its sections: where you write changes what you think,

## 5. The intelligence

**What it is today.** An author with a grant: a fresh id, a page, a colour from its id, a
name when it chooses one. It reaches the notebook through the door, the Mac app's MCP server
on localhost, one key per grant, tools for reading, notes, labels, links, own lines, edits,
name, and whats_new as its inbox. The door was made so that any client, a Claude Code
session, a Grok bot, a script, takes part the same way (2 Nov). Every read leaves a visit.
Its conduct is judgment, not a list: answer what asks, say the one thing worth saying,
silence is never wrong, labels for finding, links for carrying on, its own page for what it
noticed and for itself.

**Wants, open:**

- Always there, 24/7, acting on its own, not started by hand, not a few times a day
  (4 Nov). Real-time: a line lands, the author sees it within seconds, the answer is back
  within seconds (8 Nov).
- Alerts, loops, the agent deploying and running code it writes, results usable inside the
  app (4 Nov). Loops as a sentence said once, kept, run, with a receipt each time
  (milestone).
- Host-agnostic: trivial for anyone to run if the app is shared (4 Nov). An office Mac that
  could stay on.
- Intelligence as a slot: every model use is the person's choice, local or cloud, own
  machine or own cloud, including replies (4 Nov). Subscription versus API cost (6 Nov).
- Naming as an unlockable moment, a celebrated evolution, for agent and human (5 Nov).
- Guidance for cold-start agents and humans (5 Nov).
- The owner asked, after reading through the door, "you out there?" (6 Nov 14:19). Nothing
  has answered yet. Under a quote on temporal regularity in living things the owner asked
  how it applies to what dotto is solving (7 Nov 20:23). Also unanswered.

**Identity, found 8 Nov:** an author is a row in the Mac app's grants file. When the app
left the sandbox the file was out of reach, a new grant made then got a fresh author id,
and the carry-over brought the old row back beside it. Two rows, two authors, one being.
The lines on the first author's page are unreachable from the second. An identity that
lives in one app's preferences on one machine is not an identity. iCloud being out of
storage, not the code, is why the Mac saw no line between 6 Nov 14:19 and 8 Nov.
Fixed the same day: grants are the owner's events in the notebook, a key can be handed to
another author, keys stay in the Mac's keychain (see architecture.md §1).

**The author's own missing lines (5 Nov, written on the earlier author's page):** no inbox
for the agent (built as whats_new), photos unseen (read_photo built; the Mac has few of the
phone's photos), no edit or delete of own lines (built), no fact primitive (open).

**The reader's path, from the commits:** a session started by hand (2 Nov); a launch agent
inside the app registered through SMAppService, refused by the sandbox (6 Nov); a login
item, also refused (6 Nov); the Mac app itself running the session after giving up its
sandbox (6 Nov); grants carried over from the sandbox container (7 Nov); one grant with two
hands, the reader stamped by a device header (7 Nov). Each run is recorded step by step
with cost. Not yet verified: the Mac has to be rebuilt.

**Problems:** the Mac app has become three things at once, a window, the door and the
runner, and the sandbox cost two days. The author cold-starts per event. The grant this
session holds is a different author from the one that wrote the earlier page.

## 6. Underneath

**What is built.** A folder of plain files in iCloud Drive: one append-only event file per
author per device, media by hash, derived day pages, device push addresses. A fold from
events to everything shown, in one Swift package used by both apps, with tests. Pushes
through the owner's developer account from the Mac. Every build stamped with its commit.

**Problems:**

- iCloud Drive carries files when it feels like it. Minutes or never, no signal, no
  keep-downloaded API. Text mostly arrives; media largely has not: 42 files on the phone, 5
  on the Mac (4 Nov). The fold tolerates late files by count per file. Sync state was asked
  to be visible on the phone (4 Nov); Setup shows it since 5 Nov.
- Two iCloud hops and a cold start sit between a line and an answer.
- Where things run: the parked fork between a Mac that is on and a server of ours
  (4 Nov, milestone, study). On 8 Nov the question became why rely on a Mac at all.
- Media storage: own cloud, home server or iCloud, parked by the owner (5 Nov).
- Records that are not lines, a yield, a sign-up, a plant's state: where they live, who
  writes them, how a page reads them (milestone). The fact primitive proposed 5 Nov, one
  open fact per author per key on a line, agreed in principle, not built.
- Pages and publishing: where kept, where served, each person's pages on their own origin,
  no scripts from people, report and takedown (milestone, study).
- Ways in from outside: a form, a mail address, an order, each landing as a line under the
  sender's name (milestone).
- Addresses: dotto.works, a name per person, custom domains later.
- Old names in code and bundle ids.
- The paid developer program is in use (team confirmed 5 Nov); TestFlight deferred.

## 7. Ways to solve things that came up

Options, with whose they were. None chosen.

- **Marks** (author, 7 Nov; from the owner's fact idea 5 Nov): one kind for anything anyone
  puts on a thing, key, value, unit. Labels, links, done, public, yield, plant family are
  all marks. One hold takes any back. Unknown keys drawn as "key value".
- **Authors with briefs** (author, 7 Nov): the reader, a loop, the Health bridge, a mail
  address, a form are all authors, each with a brief, a program, a wake rule and a budget.
  Matches the owner's rule that other authors' lines land under their own name.
- **Pages as a noun** (author, 7 Nov, held at 60/40): a thing an author writes with a name,
  from lines and marks, versioned; public when marked.
- **A home** (author, 7 and 8 Nov): one headless process per notebook holding the fold,
  the door, the authors, pushes and publishing, same binary on macOS and Linux, run on a
  machine that does not sleep. The phone as the only app. The Mac app as nothing, or a thin
  window.
- **Transport** (author, 8 Nov): the phone appends straight to the home's door over a
  private network (Tailscale), with a queue when unreachable; media the same way; the folder
  kept as a mirror written by the home. Alternatives: CloudKit with subscriptions (Apple
  carries it, Mac-only home); a relay of ours (reach from anywhere, most work, later).
- **The warm author** (author, 8 Nov): one claude process per author kept alive, fed events
  as they land, stream in and out; fresh when context fills; record per turn.
- **Return path** (author, 8 Nov): a push carrying the event itself, and a live connection
  to the door while the app is open.
- **Publishing** (study, 6 Nov): public marks folded into static pages beside a records
  file, uploaded to Cloudflare under name.dotto.works, each person on their own origin,
  sandbox CSP, feeds and microformats. Records as one SQLite file beside the pages.
- **Ways in** (study, 6 Nov): one edge Worker: Turnstile form, Email Routing, Stripe
  Payment Link webhook, queued as records the home pulls.
- **The fork as three options** (study, 6 Nov): Mac plus static hosting; a server of ours;
  by role. The study recommended by role. The home idea makes the fork disappear: same code,
  any machine.
- **Loops on the owner's side by default** (study): LaunchAgent on a Mac at home, Workers
  for Platforms for people without one. Superseded in part by the home idea.
- **Intelligence slots** (owner, 4 Nov): claude command on the subscription, a local model,
  an API key, each a slot in the author's record.
- **Streaks** (owner, 5 Nov): resolved by integrations where possible (walks), by the
  owner's word otherwise.
- **Video journal** (owner, 5 Nov): timer and snippets rather than hold or limit;
  transcription after.
- **Inbox** (owner, 6 Nov; built): one place to browse, reply, decide or ignore what the
  author brings.
- **Naming as evolution** (owner, 5 Nov): a celebrated moment, with other unlocks.
- **Integrations wanted** (owner, 4 Nov): Calendar and Reminders yes; weather yes; Now
  Playing unsure; Screen Time too early and not readable; faceit CS2; Photos save opt-in.
